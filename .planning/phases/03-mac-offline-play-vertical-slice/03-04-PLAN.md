Purpose: LIBR-03 must survive a new computer, an offline edit, and a missed notification. The only mechanism in this codebase that already guarantees that is the journal/snapshot/cursor spine, so curation rides it rather than inventing a second sync path.
Output: six tables, one scoped context, one journal kind, one snapshot branch, idempotent REST intents, and contract tests for convergence and ordering.
</objective>

Implement `Playstead.Curation.Position` as a fractional index over a fixed ordered alphabet, with `first/0`, `last/1`, `between/2` accepting `nil` on either side to mean start or end of list, and `needs_rebalance?/2` reporting when two neighbours are adjacent in the encoding with no representable value between them. A conflict-free replicated ordered-list type is deliberately not used here. A personal library's queue is dozens of items with a single owner and occasional offline edits; a fractional index is one string column and a small pure module, and the discussion research records the CRDT alternative as rejected on that basis.
